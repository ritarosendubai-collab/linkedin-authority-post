In 35 days, banks lose the right to keep small businesses waiting.

On 13 September 2026, the SME Customer Protection Regulation takes effect. It was published in February. Almost nobody has prepared.

What changes on that date:

Three business days to open an SME account.
Fourteen days maximum delay — with a written reason.
No closing fee after six months.
No forced product bundles.
Sixty days' notice before terms or fees change.

Here's the part most founders miss.

The three-day clock does not start when you apply. It starts when your file is complete.

If your documents are missing or wrong, the deadline never begins to run. And where the bank is applying enhanced financial-crime checks, it is waived outright.

The obligation sits with the bank either way. You have to qualify for it.

So before 13 September, get the file clean: trade licence, constitutional documents, ID and passport for every shareholder, proof of address, and one page describing what the business does and where the money comes from.

That last document is where most applications fail.

I have watched founders lose months waiting on an account with no explanation. That there is now a legal deadline on it, no exit fee, and a named body to escalate to tells you something about how seriously this market takes the people building in it.
