# Tone Calibration

## The Problem

Professional registers differ by market. A line that reads as warm and appropriate in one business culture reads as either gushing or grudging in another — and the writer usually cannot hear it, because they are writing in their own register.

The most common failure is a writer trained in an understated register writing for a market with a warmer one. In business cultures where enthusiasm is discounted — much of UK and US corporate communication — praise is deliberately restrained, and restraint signals sincerity. In markets where warmth about institutions and country is conventional and expected, that same restrained line does not read as measured. It reads as faint praise, or as withholding.

The UAE and wider Gulf professional feed generally sits at the warmer end. So does much of South Asia, the Levant, and Latin America. Nordic and UK feeds sit cooler. These are conventions, not rules, and they vary by industry and by individual — a Gulf-based engineering firm's feed may read cooler than a London agency's.

**So do not assume from the market label.** Look at the actual feed: the user's own previous posts, their network's posts, the comments under similar content. Calibrate to what is observably normal there. If you cannot see the feed, ask the user how the rest of their feed sounds — they know, even if they have never articulated it.

## The Three-Temperature Method

Never silently pick a register for someone else's voice. Offer three, ordered, with a recommendation and the reasoning. This is a required human choice point.

**Warmer** — direct, positive, institutionally generous. Names the achievement plainly.

**Warmer still** — superlative and explicitly comparative. Positions the change against other markets. Works when it is genuinely true and the writer has standing to say it; reads as promotional when either is missing.

**Personal** — the same praise routed through the writer's own experience. Almost always the strongest.

Present them as a block, let the user pick, and record the choice in `decision-packet.md`.

## Why Personal Usually Wins

A generic compliment about an institution can be written by anyone, which is exactly why it reads as promotional and generates no replies. The same sentiment grounded in something only the writer has seen becomes credible — and it gives the network something to respond to, because people reply to experience far more readily than to assessment.

The pattern: an observed problem the writer has personally watched, then the change, then what the change signals, then optionally why it matters to them.

> I have watched founders lose months waiting on an account with no explanation. That there is now a legal deadline on it, no exit fee, and a named ombudsman tells you something about how seriously this market takes the people building in it.

That is warmer than the superlative version despite using no superlatives, because it costs the writer something to say.

## Placement

A considered positive line lands better near the end than at the start.

At the opening it reads as throat-clearing before the real content. Placed after the practical checklist and before the closing question, it functions as a considered close — the writer has done the critical work first and is giving credit having earned the right to. It also warms the reader immediately before you ask them a pointed question, which measurably helps the reply rate.

## Guardrails

- **Only if true.** Do not manufacture praise for a regulation that is weak. If the honest read is that it is a modest improvement, say that instead. A post that praises everything is worth nothing.
- **Praise the change, not the poster's own market position.** The line is about the regulation. The moment it becomes about the writer's business it stops working.
- **No comparative claims without grounds.** "Few markets do this" is a factual claim. Either it is checkable and you have checked, or soften it to something you can stand behind.
- **Match the writer's existing voice.** If their previous posts are dry and analytical, the warmest option will read as someone else wrote it — which, in this workflow, would be true. Calibrate to their range, not the market's maximum.
- **Political caution.** Praising a regulator or a policy is normal professional commentary. Praising a government's broader political direction is a different act. Stay with the specific change.
